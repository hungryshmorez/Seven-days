# SEVEN DAYS — Story Summary (for the tape agent)

This is a plain summary of the story. The master bible (`reference/SEVEN_DAYS_MASTER_BIBLE.md`) is the full authority. If anything here disagrees with the bible, the bible wins.

## The core idea

The protagonist has been in a coma the whole time. The search for friends, the island, the seven days and the tapes are a dream inside that coma. The protagonist believes they are trying to bring friends home and escape. In truth, each escape attempt carries them deeper into the coma.

Every character is part of the protagonist's own mind. The friends were at the hospital. This is never announced early; it is hinted through medical sounds, accident reports, repeated sirens and memory gaps.

## The friends

- **Mitzi Goins**: calm and organised, the map-holder. Taken behind the mall shutter on Day One.
- **Alan**: the movie guy with a camera. Hurt in the crash. Lost to the warehouse screens on Day Two.
- **Sloppy**: an 80s rodeo clown with a yellow hat. Lost in the crash on the island.
- **Oliver**: a priest and glitch exploiter, sardonic. Lost in the broken level on Day Three.

**Dead friends stay dead.** Sloppy is lost in the prologue; do not show him alive afterward in a tape.

**Lolo and Yuri** are original helpers (one body, two heads). They are not friends from the recordings.

## The seven days (in order)

**Prologue — The Island.** Six people board a small charter plane: the protagonist and four friends. The engine fails and the plane goes down on a remote island. Sloppy is lost in the wreckage. The survivors wake on the shore; Alan is hurt. A radio says "day one of seven." At a campsite the protagonist finds a tape. A hunter called the Cleaverback chases them into a giant hole that drops into a bunker. In the bunker a tape plays on a television and SPLICEMASTER says, "There you are. We've been waiting."

**Day One — The Abandoned Mall (Hollowmere Galleria).** The forest road leads to an empty shopping mall. Shuttered shops, a dry fountain, dead escalators, a children's playhouse. A directory reads "YOU ARE HERE / NO EXITS ON THIS LEVEL." A terminal says: "THE SYSTEM DOES NOT KILL USERS. IT REASSIGNS THEM." A marionette called Morrow Moppet takes Mitzi behind the playhouse shutter. She is missing. The protagonist flees through the food court; the PA repeats the rule.

**Day Two — The Nostalgia Warehouse.** Shelves of childhood memories on glowing screens: "These are my memories." "They belong to us now." A false exit opens onto a sunlit campsite that slams shut. The Cleaverback returns: "You didn't get away. You just changed channels." A childhood bedroom where SPLICEMASTER says, "The best memories are the ones we can use again." Alan's voice comes from a screen, then stops. Alan is missing. The protagonist descends in a lift labelled RENDER DISTANCE.

**Day Three — Render Distance.** An unfinished game world with checkerboard floors and frozen objects. Signs: "WELCOME TO THE LAST LEVEL / YOU HAVE ALREADY LOST." A blue runner (the Velocity Wraith) says, "The world hasn't finished loading." A corrupted guide called Fennix warns, "He can hear your footsteps." Oliver is lost in a broken section of the level. The protagonist is then pulled into a home-dream: a familiar house, news reports about the accident, a missing-person flyer, a lost child who asks for help, and finally a takeover by the Burned Accomplice that leads toward a burning pentagram. An unnamed figure in white says: "Four stones. North, south, east, west. If the fire goes out, the attempt fails." The protagonist falls through a door marked **404 — WORLD NOT FOUND**.

**Day Four — 404 Forest / One Long Night.** One continuous night, not a campaign compressed into one night. The clock runs from 11:59 PM to 3:07 AM. The forest loops and contradicts itself: a river that returns to its source, furniture on the ceiling, backward footprints, a voice counting breaths, total silence, recordings hanging from branches. The radio warns, "Do not follow voices from the treeline." A copy of the protagonist's voice says, "I'm outside. Open the door." A giant with a loudspeaker head says, "Remain where you are." A ribbon-bodied guide called Pale Thread leads the protagonist to a root tunnel. A scratched warning reads: **"THEY ARE NOT ALL HUNTERS. SOME REMEMBER WHAT HAPPENED."** A countdown leads to a cartoon-decorated door.

**Day Five — Everybody Smiles Here.** Two parts, one long day.

- *The crawlspace:* bodies in the dark and a man who speaks to them as friends. The Burned Accomplice chases the protagonist, who is captured and taken to a hatch room. A television there shows the prologue plane crash. A flat voice says, "Watch the whole thing. You missed the part where you lived."
- *The ritual (non-violent):* in a basement, the protagonist places stones and a voice gives instructions. Father, Son, and Holy Ghost seals are made. The sun rises. One last deep cry. The protagonist is blinded and collapses. If the attempt fails, the stones must be placed again, with no game over.
- *The studio:* a painted cartoon street. Grin Cat blocks the way: "Leaving already? We haven't even started the show!" Hound: "You smell like the outside." Ink Man: "Every good story needs a permanent cast." A film editor: "You keep ruining the ending." "Then stop writing it." The film strip tears.

**Day Six — The Broadcast.** A children's show set. Miss Merrily, a controlling presenter: "Hi, friend! Are you ready to learn something new?" Three doors each give a wrong answer ("Try again."). Buttonbell, a nervous puppet, warns: "Listen carefully. She doesn't control the show." Mr. Stillwell: "Someone is watching you through your own eyes." A facility of animatronics: "You will be remembered forever." A control room displays **THE SPLICEMASTERING PROCESS / INPUT: HUMAN MEMORY / PROCESS: RECURSIVE SIMULATION / OUTPUT: PERMANENT CHARACTER**. "Trapped? No. Preserved." The protagonist smashes the console. The sets collapse into a final channel and a countdown.

**Day Seven — The Sub-Bass Core and the endings.** A colossal column of cables and reels. SPLICEMASTER: "You made it." "Most people break before the third day." "You didn't find the tape. The tape found you." The final offer: "No more running. No more fear." The protagonist refuses: "Then I'd rather break the whole fucking thing." SPLICEMASTER mutates: "Don't turn it off!"

## The endings

**First cycle (the living-room ending).** The camera cuts to white static. The protagonist wakes in a clean living room with uninjured hands. The cassette reads **SEVEN DAYS / PLEASE WATCH UNTIL THE END.** SPLICEMASTER: "Welcome back." The on-screen text, in order:

> INSTALLING NEW HOST… / MEMORY TRANSFER COMPLETE. / IDENTITY REASSIGNED. / INSTALLATION SUCCESSFUL. / PLAY AGAIN?

Then the loop: the protagonist wakes on the beach asking, "Am I already dead?" All four friends return as hostile nightmare counterparts.

**First cycle — the hospital is fatal.** Reaching the hospital on the first run causes death.

**Second cycle — the true ending is the hospital.** The living room is now fatal. The hospital reveals the truth: staff decide to withdraw life support. "He's just too far gone." "He wouldn't have wanted this." The protagonist screams and no one hears. The family had given him seven more days; everyone begged him to wake up. Keep this restrained and human, not gory.

**Hidden survival ending (idle).** Only if the player stays completely still at the very start for a long time: a doctor says, "I've got a pulse," and the protagonist wakes in hospital alive. This is not a normal route.

## Tone and content rules for the tapes

- Found-footage look: bodycam POV, scan lines, grain. Heavy corruption only at story breaks.
- Horror is earned: quiet build, one scare, release. Never three jumps back to back.
- Unidentified frightening figures are shown as **???** and are never named.
- Ritual content stays non-violent and implied. Do not add real-world instructions.
- Dialogue is captions only; no synthesized voice.
